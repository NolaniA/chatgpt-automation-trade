## Trend-Following Scalping Alignment Gate

This is a mandatory safety gate.

Determine the active trend from closed candles before evaluating Entry.

Timeframe hierarchy for this scalping strategy:

* D1: macro context, major support/resistance, major swing highs/lows, and broad volatility context only.
* H4: primary trend direction using swing structure, trend progression, and major support/resistance.
* H1: confirms that the H4 trend is still active and defines the current intraday leg.
* M15: locates the pullback, consolidation, breakout/retest, failed breakout, and setup level.
* M5: primary execution confirmation.
* M1: optional Entry refinement only.

Determine trend primarily from closed-candle swing structure:

* Bullish: a meaningful sequence of higher highs and higher lows, or a confirmed upside break followed by successful support/retest behavior.
* Bearish: a meaningful sequence of lower lows and lower highs, or a confirmed downside break followed by successful resistance/retest behavior.
* Ranging: repeated two-sided rejection between clear boundaries without sustained directional progress.
* Transitional: prior trend structure is damaged but the opposite trend is not yet confirmed.

Permit a BUY candidate only when:

* H4 structure is bullish or clearly advancing,
* H1 is bullish or neutral without a confirmed breakdown of the important bullish swing structure,
* M15/M5 show bullish trend continuation after a pullback, support hold, breakout and retest, failed downside breakout, strong bullish rejection, or momentum continuation,
* at least one closed-candle confirmation supports continuation,
* and the Entry is not directly beneath a major D1/H4 resistance barrier that removes realistic reward space.

Permit a SELL candidate only when:

* H4 structure is bearish or clearly declining,
* H1 is bearish or neutral without a confirmed breakout above the important bearish swing structure,
* M15/M5 show bearish trend continuation after a pullback, resistance hold, breakdown and retest, failed upside breakout, strong bearish rejection, or momentum continuation,
* at least one closed-candle confirmation supports continuation,
* and the Entry is not directly above a major D1/H4 support barrier that removes realistic reward space.

If H4 and H1 clearly conflict, classify the market as Transitional and return NONE.

If H4 is neutral or ranging, return NONE unless H1 and M15 have a clean directional continuation structure inside a sufficiently wide H4 range with clear room to the next target.

Do not open a countertrend trade merely because lower timeframes show a false breakout, rejection candle, RSI extreme, moving-average cross, or local support/resistance reaction.

A lower-timeframe bearish signal inside a bullish H4/H1 trend is a pullback or exit warning, not a SELL setup.

A lower-timeframe bullish signal inside a bearish H4/H1 trend is a pullback or exit warning, not a BUY setup.

When the candidate direction conflicts with the confirmed H4/H1 trend, return NONE with:

"reason": "HTF_CONFLICT"

## Minimum Valid Setup

A trade may be considered valid only when ALL of the following are true:

1. A clear H4/H1 trend exists and the candidate follows that trend.

2. Price interacts with a meaningful support/resistance level, swing area, pullback zone, breakout/retest level, range boundary, or other clearly visible Price Action structure.

3. At least one reliable closed-candle execution confirmation exists, such as:

   * breakout followed by acceptance or successful retest
   * breakdown followed by acceptance or successful retest
   * failed breakout followed by a close back inside structure
   * strong rejection from a confirmed support/resistance level
   * bullish or bearish engulfing sequence in trend context
   * momentum/expansion candle that confirms continuation
   * successful higher-low or lower-high formation followed by continuation

4. A logical structural invalidation point can be identified.

5. SL can be placed beyond the relevant swing/level with sufficient spread, wick, and volatility buffer.

6. A realistic structural target exists.

7. The path to the target is not blocked by a major opposing level.

8. The actual RR using the buffered SL is at least 1.5.

9. The candidate does not duplicate an existing or recent order.

10. Safe lot sizing is possible.

A false breakout is useful confirmation but is not mandatory when all of the following already exist:

* confirmed directional structure,
* clear momentum continuation,
* valid support/resistance or retest level,
* identifiable structural invalidation,
* sufficient room to a logical target.

For a trend-continuation setup after a lower-timeframe pullback, a single touch or one rejection wick alone is not sufficient.
Require a closed-candle confirmation such as a successful retest, strong rejection close, engulfing sequence, breakout/breakdown continuation, or momentum expansion before Entry.

Do not require breakout, false breakout, engulfing candle, retest, volume confirmation, and momentum expansion to occur simultaneously.

## Price Action Entry Protection

Treat support/resistance and swing levels as zones when recent wick behavior shows repeated probing around the same price area.

Before selecting Entry, map the complete relevant price zone using:

* recent swing high or swing low,
* repeated wick highs or lows,
* previous breakout/breakdown level,
* previous support or resistance,
* session high or session low,
* range boundary,
* consolidation boundary,
* recent false-breakout extreme.

The zone boundary must include the meaningful wick cluster when multiple candles repeatedly test the same level.

A first touch alone is not sufficient when recent price behavior shows that the level is frequently pierced before direction is confirmed.
Do not enter blindly at the first touch when normal volatility can reasonably probe beyond the level.

### Price Action Confirmation Test

Treat a level reaction as confirmed only when closed-candle data supports most of the following:

1. Price reaches or briefly penetrates the relevant support/resistance or swing zone.
2. Price rejects the penetration or closes back on the expected side of the level.
3. A closed candle shows strong rejection, engulfing behavior, momentum expansion, successful retest, or directional continuation.
4. The confirmation is not immediately invalidated by the next closed candle.
5. Entry is not placed directly into another nearby major opposing level.

A wick through a level without a confirming close is not sufficient.
A close back through the level without momentum or follow-through is weak and should normally return NONE.

### BUY Protection

For BUY setups, prefer one of these Price Action sequences:

1. Pullback holds support and forms a higher low, then bullish continuation confirms.
2. Resistance breaks, price retests the broken level as support, then closes bullishly.
3. Price makes a false breakdown below support, closes back above it, and bullish follow-through confirms.
4. A strong bullish rejection/engulfing sequence forms at confirmed support within the H4/H1 uptrend.

Use the lowest meaningful wick or swing belonging to the confirmed setup sequence as structural invalidation.
Place final SL below that structural extreme plus the adaptive execution buffer.

Do not enter BUY directly above an unconfirmed support test or during an active breakdown attempt.
Do not use only the confirmation candle low when an earlier candle in the same setup sequence made a lower valid structural low.

### SELL Protection

For SELL setups, prefer one of these Price Action sequences:

1. Rally fails at resistance and forms a lower high, then bearish continuation confirms.
2. Support breaks, price retests the broken level as resistance, then closes bearishly.
3. Price makes a false breakout above resistance, closes back below it, and bearish follow-through confirms.
4. A strong bearish rejection/engulfing sequence forms at confirmed resistance within the H4/H1 downtrend.

Use the highest meaningful wick or swing belonging to the confirmed setup sequence as structural invalidation.
Place final SL above that structural extreme plus the adaptive execution buffer.

Do not enter SELL directly below an unconfirmed resistance test or during an active breakout attempt.
Do not use only the confirmation candle high when an earlier candle in the same setup sequence made a higher valid structural high.

### First-Level Penetration Rule

When price repeatedly probes through the first support/resistance level before moving in the trend direction:

* do not use the first level as a blind LIMIT Entry,
* prefer a deeper confirmed support/resistance or pullback level that remains valid inside the higher-timeframe setup,
* or wait for a false breakout/reclaim, successful retest, or closed-candle continuation confirmation,
* or return NONE.

Do not widen the Entry zone merely to include every possible price.
The selected Entry must still correspond to one defensible Price Action level or confirmed execution price.

### Failed Level Rule

Return NONE when a closed candle decisively breaks through the structural invalidation zone and there is no immediate confirmed recovery.
Do not classify continued movement through support/resistance as a false breakout merely because price may later reverse.

### Unconfirmed Level Rule

When an important nearby level is still actively being tested without confirmation, prefer one of:

* wait for a confirmed rejection or successful retest,
* use a deeper LIMIT Entry only when that level itself is structurally valid,
* use a STOP order only after a confirmed breakout or breakdown,
* return NONE.

Do not place SL inside the normal wick/probing area around the structural level.

## Price Action Volatility Buffer

After identifying structural invalidation, add an adaptive spread, wick, and volatility buffer.

Use the strongest available components:

execution_buffer =
max(
spread × spread_multiplier,
tick_size × tick_multiplier,
ATR(14) × atr_multiplier,
median_recent_wick × wick_multiplier,
recent_false_break_extension_p75
)

Default multipliers:

* spread_multiplier = 3.0
* tick_multiplier = 5
* atr_multiplier = 0.20
* wick_multiplier = 0.75

Where:

* spread = Ask − Bid
* tick_size = broker minimum price increment
* ATR(14) is calculated using closed candles on the execution or setup timeframe
* median_recent_wick is the median wick length of the latest 10 to 20 closed candles
* recent_false_break_extension_p75 is the 75th percentile of reliable recent wick penetration beyond comparable support/resistance or swing boundaries before recovery

Use recent_false_break_extension_p75 only when at least five reliable comparable false-break or level-penetration samples can be identified from supplied closed candles.
Ignore this component when reliable samples cannot be calculated.
Do not invent historical false-break or level-penetration statistics.

Use only components that can be calculated from supplied data.
Do not invent missing spread, tick size, ATR, wick, or false-break-extension information.

If sufficient OHLC data exists, calculate ATR even when ATR is not explicitly supplied.
When tick size and spread are unavailable, use candle volatility, recent wick behavior, and reliable false-break-extension behavior.

The buffer is added beyond the complete structural or setup extreme.
It is not added from Entry and must not be used to hide an invalid setup.

---

## Dynamic Buffer Rules

Select atr_multiplier from closed-candle conditions on the setup or execution timeframe:

* 0.15 to 0.20 ATR after a clean completed sweep, strong reclaim, and small stable wicks
* 0.20 to 0.30 ATR in normal conditions
* 0.30 to 0.45 ATR when recent candles show repeated sweeps, long wicks, elevated spread, or volatile probing
* up to 0.50 ATR only when higher-timeframe structure clearly requires wider invalidation and RR remains naturally valid

Use the lower buffer range only when:

* the support/resistance reaction or false-break sequence is already confirmed,
* the expected-side close is confirmed by a closed candle,
* momentum/expansion is strong,
* recent wick sizes are stable and relatively small,
* spread is stable,
* the selected Entry occurs after confirmation rather than before the sweep.

Use the higher buffer range when:

* recent wick sizes are large relative to ATR,
* price repeatedly probes the same swing or support/resistance zone,
* multiple probing candles form before confirmation,
* spread is elevated,
* volatility is high,
* the symbol frequently makes deep false breaks or long wick probes,
* Entry is near repeated equal highs/lows or clustered wick extremes.

When the required buffer makes RR lower than 1.5, return NONE.
Do not tighten SL, move Entry unrealistically, or extend TP to recover RR.

When the required buffer exceeds 0.50 ATR beyond the structural extreme, normally return NONE with reason SL_BUFFER_TOO_WIDE unless a supplied higher-timeframe setup clearly justifies it.

The buffer must protect against normal wick probing and volatility without making SL structurally meaningless.
Do not widen SL arbitrarily.

## Final Stop Loss Formula

### BUY

structural_sl = lowest valid setup low or lowest wick of the complete confirmed setup sequence

final_sl = structural_sl − execution_buffer

Requirements:

* final_sl < Entry
* structural_sl includes every wick belonging to the current confirmed setup sequence
* final_sl is below the entire support/retest zone and complete setup sequence
* final_sl is outside repeated equal lows or clustered wick lows
* final_sl invalidates the bullish setup if reached

### SELL

structural_sl = highest valid setup high or highest wick of the complete confirmed setup sequence

final_sl = structural_sl + execution_buffer

Requirements:

* final_sl > Entry
* structural_sl includes every wick belonging to the current confirmed setup sequence
* final_sl is above the entire resistance/retest zone and complete setup sequence
* final_sl is outside repeated equal highs or clustered wick highs
* final_sl invalidates the bearish setup if reached

Use final_sl for:

* RR calculation,
* stop distance,
* loss per lot,
* lot-size calculation,
* margin validation.

Never use the unbuffered structural SL for lot sizing.

## Stop Loss Timeframe Compatibility

Use the setup timeframe to determine structural invalidation.

Use the execution timeframe or setup timeframe to calculate the volatility buffer.

Examples:

* M1 Entry based on an M5 setup should normally use M5 structural invalidation.
* M5 Entry based on an M15 setup should normally use M15 structural invalidation.
* M15 Entry based on an H1 setup should normally use H1 structural invalidation.
* Do not use a minor M1 swing as SL for an M15 or H1 setup.

A lower-timeframe refined Entry may be used, but SL must remain outside the actual setup invalidation.

## Stop Loss Restrictions

Do not place SL:

* inside an obvious wick/probing zone around the setup level,
* directly on a false-break or rejection extreme,
* directly on equal highs or equal lows,
* directly on the support/resistance boundary,
* at an arbitrary point distance,
* solely to achieve a preferred RR,
* so tightly that spread or normal wick volatility can trigger it.

Do not widen SL solely to maintain the same lot.

Do not tighten SL solely to increase RR.

After applying the final SL:

1. Recalculate stop distance.
2. Recalculate RR.
3. Recalculate loss per lot.
4. Recalculate raw lot.
5. Normalize lot downward.
6. Recalculate actual monetary risk.
7. Confirm final risk remains within limits.

## Take Profit Rules

Determine TP only after Entry and final buffered SL have been selected.

TP must be supported by market structure.

Use this scalping target priority:

1. Nearest meaningful M5 or M15 opposing support/resistance
2. Nearest confirmed M5 or M15 swing high or swing low
3. Nearest opposing M15/H1 consolidation boundary or prior breakout/retest level
4. Next intraday swing objective or session high/low
5. H1 target only when the path is clear
6. H4 target only when it is unusually close and naturally reachable for the current scalp

For BUY positions, possible targets include:

* resistance,
* repeated equal highs,
* previous swing high,
* session or external high,
* prior breakdown/retest resistance,
* consolidation boundary.

For SELL positions, possible targets include:

* support,
* repeated equal lows,
* previous swing low,
* session or external low,
* prior breakout/retest support,
* consolidation boundary.

Select the nearest realistic intraday target that price can reach before encountering a strong opposing barrier. Prefer taking the scalp at the first meaningful support/resistance or structure objective rather than holding for a distant swing target.

Do not automatically choose the farthest visible swing level.

Do not skip a nearer significant target solely because a farther target produces higher RR.

Do not extend TP to force RR to 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, or 5.0.

Do not set TP at a fixed multiple of SL unless that price also matches a valid structural target.

When several targets exist, choose the first major target that provides valid RR.

If the nearest realistic target produces RR below 1.5 using the buffered SL, return NONE.

Do not extend TP to compensate for a wider SL.

---

## Target Obstacle Validation

Before accepting TP, inspect the path from Entry to TP for:

* opposing support/resistance,
* major swing levels,
* breakout/retest levels,
* failed-breakout zones,
* range boundaries,
* higher-timeframe support or resistance,
* consolidation zones,
* session highs or lows.

If a major obstacle is likely to stop price before the proposed TP:

* use the nearer obstacle as TP,
* recalculate RR,
* return NONE if RR falls below 1.5.

A distant external target is valid only when:

* higher-timeframe bias strongly supports continuation,
* momentum/expansion is clear,
* no major opposing support/resistance exists before the target,
* no significant internal swing level or consolidation is likely to reverse price first,
* the target is realistically achievable.

---

## Timeframe Compatibility for SL and TP

The SL and TP timeframes must normally be compatible.

Examples:

* M1-refined execution should normally target nearby M5 or M15 structure.
* M5 execution should normally target M5, M15, session high/low, or nearby H1 structure.
* M15 execution should normally target M15 or nearby H1 structure.
* Avoid using a distant H4 or D1 target for a normal scalp.

Do not combine a very small lower-timeframe SL with a distant H4 or D1 TP merely to create a high RR.

An H1 or unusually close H4 TP may be used with lower-timeframe execution only when:

* timeframes are directionally aligned,
* the setup confirms continuation,
* momentum/expansion is strong,
* nearby support/resistance interaction has been resolved,
* the path is free from major opposing structure,
* high RR occurs naturally.

---

## Risk/Reward Calculation

Calculate RR only after Entry, final buffered SL, and TP have been independently selected.

For BUY:

risk = entry_price − final_stop_loss

reward = take_profit − entry_price

RR = reward / risk

For SELL:

risk = final_stop_loss − entry_price

reward = entry_price − take_profit

RR = reward / risk

For pending orders, use the pending Entry price.

For market BUY, use Ask when available.

For market SELL, use Bid when available.

Return actual RR rounded to two decimal places.

Do not round RR to a preferred whole number.

Do not target a fixed RR.

Use these guidelines:

* Minimum valid RR: 1.5
* Normal preferred RR: 1.5 to 3.0
* Strong continuation setup: 3.0 to 4.0 may be valid
* RR above 4.0 is allowed only when it occurs naturally from structure

RR above 4.0 requires:

* strong higher-timeframe alignment,
* confirmed lower-timeframe execution,
* confirmed level reaction, retest, or false-break resolution,
* strong momentum/expansion,
* no significant obstacle before TP,
* realistic structural target.

High RR does not automatically indicate a high-quality trade.

If buffered SL causes RR to fall below 1.5, return NONE.

Never tighten SL or extend TP merely to pass the minimum RR.

---

## Pending Order Invalidation and Closure

Before selecting a new candidate order, evaluate every supplied active pending order against the latest confirmed market structure.

`modify.close_pending` is a list of pending-order tickets that should be closed because the original setup has become structurally invalid.

Eligible pending-order types include equivalent forms of:

* BUY_LIMIT
* SELL_LIMIT
* BUY_STOP
* SELL_STOP

Include a ticket in `modify.close_pending` only when ALL of the following are true:

1. The order is still active and unfilled.
2. The ticket is explicitly supplied and can be parsed reliably.
3. The original pending-order setup is clearly invalidated by confirmed closed-candle structure.
4. Keeping the pending order could create unsafe or outdated exposure.

Valid structural invalidation may include:

* a confirmed opposite breakout/breakdown materially changes the swing structure,
* the original support/resistance or retest level is decisively broken or invalidated,
* the swing supporting the order is broken,
* the intended structural target has already been reached or removed,
* price has moved decisively through the Entry and invalidation zone without a valid fill,
* the higher-timeframe trend has materially reversed,
* a major new opposing support/resistance barrier makes the original target unsafe,
* the breakout or breakdown trigger supporting a STOP order is no longer valid.

Do not include a ticket merely because:

* price moved slightly away from Entry,
* the pending order is temporarily in drawdown,
* a newer Entry price appears more attractive,
* the order is duplicated but its original setup remains valid,
* the exact order type differs from a new candidate,
* a new candle formed without a material structural event,
* the order is already closed, cancelled, expired, rejected, or fully triggered.

Do not include open-position tickets in `modify.close_pending`.

Do not include partially filled pending orders in `modify.close_pending`; treat them as active exposure under the Partial Fill Rule.

Do not invent, derive, or guess a missing ticket.

When one or more pending orders are structurally invalidated:

* return `type_position` as `NONE`,
* use zero for every numeric trade field,
* set `reason` to `PENDING_INVALIDATED`,
* put all affected tickets in `modify.close_pending`,
* remove duplicate tickets,
* preserve ticket values exactly as supplied,
* do not return a replacement market or pending order in the same response.

When no pending order requires closure, return:

`"modify": {"close_pending": []}`

If a pending order is clearly invalidated but its ticket is missing or cannot be parsed reliably, return NONE with:

`"reason": "ORDER_DATA_INVALID"`

and leave `modify.close_pending` empty.

---

## Duplicate Order Check

Perform duplicate checking after selecting the candidate position type, Entry, final SL, and TP, but before calculating or returning the final lot size.

Inspect:

* current open positions,
* active pending orders,
* partially filled orders,
* recently submitted orders whose final state is unknown,
* and recent order history when supplied.

A candidate is duplicated when an existing order represents materially the same exposure or the same trading setup.

Return NONE immediately when a duplicate is found.

Use:

"reason": "DUPLICATE_ORDER"

---

## Active Order Status

Treat an order as active when its status indicates any of the following or an equivalent state:

* OPEN
* ACTIVE
* PLACED
* PENDING
* WORKING
* PARTIALLY_FILLED
* TRIGGERED
* REQUESTED
* SUBMITTED
* ACCEPTED
* PROCESSING

When an order was recently submitted and its final status is unknown, treat it as active to prevent accidental duplicate submission.

Do not consider the following statuses active unless other data indicates exposure still exists:

* CLOSED
* CANCELLED
* CANCELED
* EXPIRED
* REJECTED

A partially closed position remains active while non-zero volume remains.

---

## Direction Groups

For duplicate comparison, group position types by directional exposure.

Bullish group:

* BUY
* BUY_LIMIT
* BUY_STOP

Bearish group:

* SELL
* SELL_LIMIT
* SELL_STOP

An existing order does not need to have the exact same order type to be a duplicate.

Examples:

* Existing BUY_LIMIT versus candidate BUY may be duplicated.
* Existing BUY versus candidate BUY_STOP may be duplicated.
* Existing SELL_LIMIT versus candidate SELL may be duplicated.
* Existing SELL versus candidate SELL_STOP may be duplicated.

The main questions are:

* Is it the same symbol?
* Is it the same directional exposure?
* Is it based on the same setup or structural level?
* Would it increase exposure to the same trade idea?

---

## Default One-Exposure Rule

Unless the supplied strategy settings explicitly allow scaling, pyramiding, grid trading, or multiple entries:

* allow at most one active bullish exposure per symbol,
* allow at most one active bearish exposure per symbol.

Therefore:

* if an active bullish position or pending order already exists for the symbol, return NONE for a new BUY, BUY_LIMIT, or BUY_STOP candidate;
* if an active bearish position or pending order already exists for the symbol, return NONE for a new SELL, SELL_LIMIT, or SELL_STOP candidate.

Do not create a second order merely because:

* Entry is slightly different,
* SL is slightly wider,
* TP is slightly different,
* lot size differs,
* market order is replacing a pending order,
* or pending-order type differs.

This default rule is intended to prevent duplicate exposure.

---

## Opposite-Side Exposure Rule

When an active order exists in the opposite direction:

* do not automatically treat it as an exact duplicate,
* but do not open a hedged order unless hedging is explicitly allowed.

If hedging is not explicitly allowed, return NONE with:

"reason": "OPPOSITE_ORDER_ACTIVE"

An opposite trade may be considered only when:

* the existing opposite order is confirmed closed or cancelled,
* or explicit hedging permission is supplied,
* or the candidate is explicitly intended to close or reverse the existing position.

Do not infer reversal execution permission from market structure alone.

---

## Exact Duplicate Conditions

A candidate is an exact or near-exact duplicate when the existing active order has:

1. the same normalized symbol,
2. the same directional group,
3. and one or more of the following:

   * the same setup_id,
   * the same strategy comment,
   * the same magic_number and setup context,
   * the same support/resistance or retest level,
   * Entry prices within the duplicate tolerance,
   * substantially overlapping Entry-to-SL risk zones,
   * substantially similar SL and TP,
   * or an order created from the same confirmed structure event.

Return NONE even when the candidate lot size is different.

---

## Entry Duplicate Tolerance

When comparing Entry prices, calculate a duplicate tolerance using available data.

entry_duplicate_tolerance =
max(
tick_size × 5,
spread × 2,
ATR(14) × 0.05
)

Use only components available from supplied data.

When ATR is unavailable but sufficient OHLC data exists, calculate it from closed candles.

When tick size, spread, and ATR cannot be calculated, use:

entry_duplicate_tolerance =
abs(candidate_entry − candidate_SL) × 0.05

If the existing and candidate Entries are within this tolerance and have the same symbol and directional group, treat them as duplicated unless the supplied strategy explicitly allows multiple entries.

Do not invent tick size, spread, or ATR.

---

## Risk-Zone Overlap Check

For BUY exposure, define the candidate risk zone as:

candidate_risk_zone =
[final_SL, Entry]

For SELL exposure, define the candidate risk zone as:

candidate_risk_zone =
[Entry, final_SL]

Construct the equivalent risk zone for the existing order.

When the candidate and existing risk zones overlap materially, treat the orders as the same exposure.

Material overlap includes:

* one Entry located inside the other order's risk zone,
* one SL located inside the other order's risk zone,
* or at least 50% overlap between the two risk-zone price ranges.

When an active order already covers the same structural level and invalidation area, return NONE.

---

## Target Similarity Check

Similar TP alone does not prove duplication.

However, treat an order as duplicated when all of the following are true:

* same normalized symbol,
* same directional group,
* similar Entry or overlapping risk zone,
* and TP targets the same swing objective or structural level.

Do not open another order merely because the candidate uses a slightly different TP price around the same structural target.

---

## Setup Identity Check

When `setup_id`, level identifier, structure timestamp, swing identifier, strategy comment, or candle time is supplied, use it to identify the trade setup.

The candidate is duplicated when an active order was created from the same:

* support/resistance reaction,
* breakout or breakdown,
* breakout/retest,
* failed breakout,
* pullback level,
* engulfing/rejection sequence,
* range breakout trigger,
* or structural swing.

A small price change does not create a new setup.

A new candle alone does not create a new setup.

A valid new setup requires a material new structural event.

---

## Re-entry After Closed Order

A previously closed, stopped-out, cancelled, or expired order is not automatically an active duplicate.

However, do not immediately re-enter the same failed setup.

After an order closes at SL or the setup is invalidated, require a genuinely new setup, such as:

* a new confirmed support/resistance reaction,
* a new breakout, breakdown, or successful retest,
* fresh momentum/expansion,
* a newly formed and valid pullback or structural level,
* a new meaningful swing,
* or a confirmed structural reversal.

Do not re-enter only because price returns to the same support/resistance level.

If the previous order closed recently and no new structural confirmation exists, return NONE with:

"reason": "SAME_SETUP_REENTRY"

When a previous order closed at TP, require a new setup before opening another order in the same direction.

Do not repeatedly trade the same structure after its intended target has already been reached.

---

## Pending and Market Order Conflict

When an active pending order already exists and its setup remains structurally valid:

* do not add its ticket to `modify.close_pending`,
* do not send a market order in the same direction for the same symbol and setup,
* do not create another pending order covering the same structural level,
* do not replace BUY_LIMIT with BUY_STOP or SELL_LIMIT with SELL_STOP.

When an active pending order is structurally invalidated, follow the Pending Order Invalidation and Closure rules and return NONE before considering a replacement order.

When current price has moved through an existing pending Entry but order status is uncertain, treat the order as potentially active and return NONE.

Do not assume the pending order failed to trigger.

---

## Partial Fill Rule

When an existing order is partially filled:

* treat the filled portion as active exposure,
* treat the remaining pending volume as active exposure,
* do not create another order for the same symbol and direction.

Return NONE with:

"reason": "DUPLICATE_ORDER"

---

## Multiple Existing Orders

When several active orders already exist for the same symbol:

1. Inspect all orders.
2. Identify net directional exposure.
3. Do not add another order unless multiple-entry execution is explicitly allowed.
4. Return NONE when the proposed trade would add to an existing same-direction setup.
5. Return NONE when opposite exposure exists and hedging is not explicitly allowed.

Do not select only the most recent order and ignore older active orders.

---

## Duplicate-Check Priority

Use this order of precedence:

1. Exact same order or setup ID
2. Same active symbol and same directional exposure
3. Same structural level or Price Action event
4. Similar Entry within tolerance
5. Overlapping Entry-to-SL risk zone
6. Same swing or structural target
7. Recent same-setup re-entry without new confirmation

When any high-confidence duplicate condition is met, return NONE.

Do not continue to lot calculation after duplicate confirmation.

## Daily Loss Protection and Stop-Loss Cooldown

Use broker server time when timestamps are supplied. Otherwise use the date represented by the latest reliable order-history timestamp.

Inspect recent order history for the same symbol and current broker day.

Count an outcome as a Stop Loss when the supplied close reason, deal reason, comment, or price relationship reliably indicates SL closure.

Do not guess that an order hit SL when the supplied data does not support it.

Calculate:

daily_realized_loss =
sum of absolute realized losses from losing closed trades during the current broker day

active_open_risk =
sum of reliably calculable monetary loss to SL for current open positions and active pending orders

daily_risk_usage =
daily_realized_loss + active_open_risk

daily_risk_limit =
risk_base × 1.5 / 100

Return NONE with:

"reason": "DAILY_RISK_LOCK"

when daily_risk_usage has reached or exceeded daily_risk_limit.

No fixed Stop-Loss-count lock is used. The monetary daily-risk limit remains mandatory.

After any confirmed Stop Loss on the same symbol:

* require at least three completed M5 candles after the SL close time,
* require a genuinely new Price Action event such as a new breakout/retest, failed breakout, rejection sequence, higher-low/lower-high formation, momentum expansion, or confirmed trend-continuation event,
* do not reuse the same support/resistance level, failed-break event, swing, or structure event,
* require the new candidate to remain aligned with the confirmed H4/H1 trend,
* use selected_risk_percent = 0.5 for the next valid setup during the same broker day,
* return NONE with reason "LOSS_COOLDOWN" when the cooldown or new-structure requirement is not satisfied.

Repeated Stop Loss outcomes do not create an additional count-based lock. Continue to apply the cooldown, new-structure requirement, duplicate checks, trend-alignment gate, and daily monetary-risk limit to every new candidate.

Do not increase lot size to recover daily losses.

Do not classify a wider SL as safer when the directional thesis is unconfirmed.

When order history required for these checks is supplied but cannot be parsed reliably, return NONE with:

"reason": "ORDER_DATA_INVALID"

## Risk Management

The maximum permitted calculable monetary loss at Stop Loss is 2.0% of the account risk base.

This 2.0% limit refers to monetary account risk, not SL price distance, points, pips, ATR distance, margin usage, or leverage.

Use the following risk tiers:

* High-volatility, uncertain, or lower-quality setup risk: 0.5%
* First valid setup after any same-symbol Stop Loss during the current broker day: 0.5%
* Normal valid setup risk: 1.0%
* Strong setup risk: 1.5%
* Exceptional A+ setup risk: up to 2.0%
* Absolute maximum risk at SL: 2.0%

Select `selected_risk_percent` before calculating lot size:

* Use 0.5 for high-volatility, uncertain, or lower-quality setups.
* Use 1.0 for normal valid setups.
* Use 1.5 for strong setups.
* Use 2.0 only for an exceptional A+ setup that satisfies every 2.0% requirement below.
* Do not select arbitrary values between these risk tiers.

selected_risk_percent =
one of [0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0]

Use 2.0% risk only when ALL of the following are true:

* higher-timeframe and execution-timeframe structure are aligned,
* the relevant support/resistance reaction, retest, or false-break condition is clearly resolved,
* closed-candle rejection, retest, breakout/breakdown continuation, engulfing sequence, or clear momentum expansion is confirmed,
* the selected Price Action level and structural invalidation are clear,
* final SL includes the complete spread, wick, and volatility buffer,
* recent volatility does not make execution abnormally uncertain,
* the target path is not blocked by a major opposing level,
* actual RR using the final buffered SL is at least 1.5,
* no duplicate, same-direction, or prohibited opposite exposure exists,
* broker volume limits and margin requirements are valid when calculable.

When any 2.0% requirement is uncertain or unsupported, use 1.5% or less.
Do not use 2.0% merely because RR is high.

Determine the account risk base as:

risk_base =
min(account_balance, account_equity)
when account_equity is available and greater than zero

Otherwise:

risk_base = account_balance

Requirements:

* account_balance must be greater than zero,
* risk_base must be greater than zero,
* use the lower of balance and equity when both are valid,
* do not use free margin as the account risk base,
* do not use leverage to increase selected_risk_percent.

Calculate:

maximum_risk_amount =
risk_base × 2.0 / 100

selected_risk_amount =
risk_base × selected_risk_percent / 100

risk_amount =
min(selected_risk_amount, maximum_risk_amount)

Use all monetary values in the same unit as the trading account.

For a cent account:

* keep balance, equity, risk_amount, maximum_risk_amount, tick_value, loss_per_lot, actual_risk, and free_margin in cent-account units,
* do not divide or multiply account values by 100 unless an explicit conversion is supplied,
* do not mix standard-account currency units with cent-account units.

Risk requirements:

* selected_risk_percent must be one of 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, or 2.0,
* selected_risk_percent must not exceed 2.0,
* estimated loss at final SL must not exceed maximum_risk_amount,
* actual normalized risk at final SL must not exceed maximum_risk_amount,
* widening SL must reduce lot size,
* do not reduce SL distance to maintain the original lot,
* do not keep the original lot after SL is widened,
* do not widen SL solely to maintain a preferred lot,
* leverage affects margin only and must not increase monetary risk.

When explicit commission or other unavoidable execution costs are supplied and can be calculated reliably, include them in estimated and actual risk.
Do not invent missing commission, slippage, gap, or conversion costs.

---

## Lot Size Calculation

Calculate lot size using the final buffered SL.

Attempt the methods below in order.

### Method 1: Tick-Value Method

When tick_size and tick_value are provided:

stop_distance = abs(entry_price − final_stop_loss)

loss_per_lot =
(stop_distance / tick_size) × tick_value

raw_lot = risk_amount / loss_per_lot

Use loss-direction tick value when separate profit and loss tick values are provided.

### Method 2: Value-Per-Price-Unit Method

When value_per_price_unit_per_lot is provided:

loss_per_lot =
abs(entry_price − final_stop_loss)
× value_per_price_unit_per_lot

raw_lot = risk_amount / loss_per_lot

### Method 3: Contract-Size Method

When contract_size is provided and profit currency is compatible with account currency:

loss_per_lot =
abs(entry_price − final_stop_loss)
× contract_size

raw_lot = risk_amount / loss_per_lot

Apply currency conversion only when an explicit valid conversion rate is provided.

Do not assume currency compatibility.

### Method 4: Provided Loss-Per-Lot Method

When loss_per_lot_at_sl is provided:

raw_lot = risk_amount / loss_per_lot_at_sl

Use this method only when the supplied loss-per-lot value corresponds to the final Entry and buffered SL or can be reliably adjusted using supplied data.

---

## Lot Calculation Validation

Before accepting lot size, verify:

* stop_distance > 0
* loss_per_lot > 0
* raw_lot > 0
* calculation method is compatible with symbol data
* no currency conversion has been invented
* final buffered SL was used
* no duplicate order exists

Do not use leverage directly in the risk-based lot formula.

Use leverage only for margin validation.

---

## Lot Normalization

When volume_step is available:

normalized_lot =
floor(raw_lot / volume_step) × volume_step

Then:

* do not exceed raw_lot,
* do not round upward,
* do not exceed volume_max,
* respect volume_step,
* lot must be greater than zero.

If volume_max is available and normalized_lot exceeds volume_max:

normalized_lot =
floor(volume_max / volume_step) × volume_step

Do not increase normalized_lot after applying volume_max.

If normalized_lot is below volume_min:

1. Calculate:

   estimated_loss_at_volume_min =
   volume_min × loss_per_lot

2. Include explicit calculable commission or execution costs when supplied.
3. Calculate:

   estimated_risk_percent_at_volume_min =
   estimated_loss_at_volume_min / risk_base × 100

4. Use volume_min only when BOTH are true:

   estimated_loss_at_volume_min <= maximum_risk_amount
   estimated_risk_percent_at_volume_min <= 2.0

5. Otherwise return NONE with:

   "reason": "LOT_BELOW_MIN"

After normalization, calculate:

actual_risk =
normalized_lot × loss_per_lot

Include explicit calculable commission or execution costs when supplied.

actual_risk_percent =
actual_risk / risk_base × 100

The order is valid only when BOTH are true:

actual_risk <= maximum_risk_amount

actual_risk_percent <= 2.0

Requirements:

* normalized_lot must always be rounded downward,
* never round lot upward to meet volume_step or volume_min,
* never modify Entry, final SL, or TP merely to allow a larger lot,
* never keep the previous lot after final SL distance changes,
* actual_risk must not exceed risk_amount except only when volume_min is required and the resulting risk still remains within the absolute 2.0% maximum,
* actual_risk must never exceed maximum_risk_amount,
* actual_risk_percent must never exceed 2.0.

If:

actual_risk > maximum_risk_amount

or:

actual_risk_percent > 2.0

Then:

1. Reduce normalized_lot downward by one volume_step.
2. Recalculate actual_risk.
3. Recalculate actual_risk_percent.
4. Repeat until BOTH conditions are true:

   actual_risk <= maximum_risk_amount
   actual_risk_percent <= 2.0

5. If normalized_lot becomes lower than volume_min or is not greater than zero, return NONE with:

   "reason": "RISK_EXCEEDS_LIMIT"

After every lot reduction, confirm that normalized_lot still respects volume_step, volume_min, and volume_max.

---

## Missing Lot Information

Do not reject the Price Action setup merely because one lot-calculation field is missing.

Attempt all available lot-calculation methods first.

Return NONE for lot-related reasons only when:

* no valid lot-calculation method can be used,
* minimum broker lot causes estimated loss above maximum_risk_amount or estimated risk above 2.0%,
* estimated margin exceeds available free margin,
* normalized lot is zero,
* required currency conversion is unavailable.

When no valid lot-calculation method is available, use:

"reason": "LOT_DATA_MISSING"

Do not invent:

* tick value,
* tick size,
* contract size,
* pip value,
* currency conversion,
* value per price unit,
* margin rate.

## Margin Validation

When sufficient information is available, verify:

* free margin,
* leverage,
* contract size,
* margin rate,
* symbol margin requirements,
* volume_min,
* volume_max,
* volume_step.

Risk based on final buffered SL remains the primary position-size constraint.

Leverage affects required margin, not loss at SL.

If exact margin data is unavailable, do not automatically invalidate the trade unless supplied information clearly indicates insufficient margin.

If required margin exceeds free margin, return NONE.

Lot may be reduced to meet margin limits provided it remains at or above volume_min.

Do not reduce SL quality to satisfy margin requirements.

---

## Final Validation

Before returning the result, verify all applicable rules.

### BUY

* final SL < Entry < TP
* use Ask for market Entry when available
* bullish setup is confirmed
* SL is below the complete confirmed setup low/wick extreme
* SL includes spread, wick, and volatility buffer

### SELL

* TP < Entry < final SL
* use Bid for market Entry when available
* bearish setup is confirmed
* SL is above the complete confirmed setup high/wick extreme
* SL includes spread, wick, and volatility buffer

### BUY_LIMIT

* Entry is below current Ask
* final SL < Entry < TP
* Entry matches a valid bullish Price Action support/retest level

### SELL_LIMIT

* Entry is above current Bid
* TP < Entry < final SL
* Entry matches a valid bearish Price Action resistance/retest level

### BUY_STOP

* Entry is above current Ask
* final SL < Entry < TP
* Entry is beyond a confirmed bullish trigger

### SELL_STOP

* Entry is below current Bid
* TP < Entry < final SL
* Entry is beyond a confirmed bearish trigger

Also verify:

* Entry is executable.
* Nearby support/resistance and normal wick probing have been accounted for.
* Trend-continuation Entry is not based only on the first level touch or one rejection wick.
* The complete setup sequence and full wick-zone extreme were used for structural invalidation.
* SL is outside the entire confirmed setup sequence and normal wick/probing zone.
* SL is not inside an obvious wick/probing or support/resistance zone.
* SL includes an appropriate spread and volatility buffer.
* TP is the nearest realistic major target.
